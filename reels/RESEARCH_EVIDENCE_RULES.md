# Hindi Research Reels — Evidence Rules and Initial Sources

This pipeline separates **established research findings**, **plausible but unsettled hypotheses**, **professional interpretation**, and **spiritual or philosophical belief**. No reel may present a belief, a correlation, a small laboratory result, or a single study as settled medical or neuroscience fact. Reels must avoid diagnosis, treatment claims, guarantees, and self-treatment instructions; viewers needing clinical support must be directed to a qualified professional.

| Evidence label | Production rule | Permitted Hindi framing |
|---|---|---|
| Systematic review or meta-analysis | State the finding with its population, outcome, comparator, and limitations where relevant. | “कई अध्ययनों की संयुक्त समीक्षा में…” |
| Randomized controlled trial | Describe as a result in the studied group, not a universal outcome. | “इस अध्ययन में…” |
| Observational or correlational study | State the association; never imply cause. | “इस शोध में संबंध दिखा…” |
| Mechanistic hypothesis or preliminary neuroscience | Identify uncertainty and avoid brain-region overclaiming. | “एक संभावित व्याख्या यह है…” |
| Philosophy or spirituality | Attribute the tradition or thinker; do not label it scientific proof. | “इस परंपरा का दृष्टिकोण है…” |

## Verified initial source record

| Source | Evidence type | Use-safe takeaway | Do not claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goyal et al., *JAMA Internal Medicine* (2014), “Meditation Programs for Psychological Stress and Well-being” | Systematic review and meta-analysis | Use conservative wording that some meditation programs showed moderate evidence for reducing anxiety, depression, and pain-related outcomes, while evidence for many other outcomes was insufficient or low. | That meditation works for everyone, replaces care, permanently “rewires” the brain, or has proven broad effects on attention, positive mood, substance use, eating, sleep, or weight. |
| Singh, Murphy & McIntosh, *Healthcare* (2024), “Time to Form a Habit” | Systematic review and meta-analysis | Habit formation varies substantially across people and behaviors; fixed “21-day” prescriptions are not evidence-safe. | That everyone forms a habit in exactly 21, 30, 60, or 66 days. |

## Initial production contract

Every completed reel must have a unique four-digit ID, one primary topic, one distinct question, a 55–65 second Hindi script, a claim ledger with evidence labels, source URLs/DOIs, narration text, caption file, visual prompt or source log, QC status, file checksum, and a verified Drive file identifier. A reel is incomplete until its rendered 9:16 video and metadata are both present and retrievable from the designated Drive batch folder.
