# Reel 0023 Claim Ledger

| Narration claim | Evidence type | Permitted wording | Source and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role or salient social identity can change observed attention/memory patterns in a task. | Experimental finding | “बदल सकते थे” / “प्रयोगों में” | Van Bavel & Cunningham (2012) reported three experiments; an assigned spy role changed an observed other-group memory pattern. Do not generalize to every role or person. [1] |
| Identity-relevant goals can contribute to momentary value calculations. | Theoretical model | “प्रस्ताव रखता है” / “एक हिस्सा” | Berkman et al. (2017) describe a model with hypotheses and boundary conditions; it is not deterministic evidence for an individual decision. [2] |
| Motivational context can modulate attention-related ERP effects in laboratory intergroup tasks. | Laboratory finding | “कुछ lab studies में” | Montalan et al. (2011) used two intergroup task experiments. No motive reading, diagnosis, or universal causal language. [3] |
| A viewer can reflect on current goal and role. | Reflection | “पूछें” | This is a non-diagnostic reflection prompt, not an intervention or test. |

## Prohibited claims

No script element may claim that attention reveals true identity, that a social role controls attention, that a role proves bias or intent, or that changing identity cures distraction. The narration may not offer medical, legal, or diagnostic advice.

## References

[3] Montalan, B., et al. (2011). *Social identity-based motivation modulates attention bias toward negative information: An event-related brain potential study.* Socioaffective Neuroscience & Psychology, 1, 5892. https://doi.org/10.3402/snp.v1i0.5892
