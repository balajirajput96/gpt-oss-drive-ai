# Reel 0026 Claim Ledger

| Narration proposition | Label | Source boundary | Required qualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Science asks which practice, population, and measure are being studied. | Methodological framing | Scientific operationalisation; no result asserted. [1] | “विज्ञान पूछता है” rather than “science proves.” |
| A label such as mindfulness can have differing definitions, practices, and measurements. | Scholarly review | Definitions/measurement vary across domains and studies. [1] [2] | Do not imply one correct universal definition. |
| A single study informs a bounded measure, not the final meaning of an entire tradition. | Epistemic boundary | Inference is constrained by operational definition and study context. [1] [2] | Do not deny or affirm a tradition’s meaning. |
| Some Buddhist traditions join meditation to wider ethical/contextual concerns. | Traditional/scholarly interpretation | Traditions are plural and not reducible to attention alone. [2] [3] | Say “कुछ बौद्ध परंपराएँ”; never generalise to all Buddhism. |
| Scientific studies do not by themselves establish or refute tradition-level meanings. | Philosophical-methodological boundary | This is a limit of scope, not empirical proof. | No “science disproves/proves Buddhism” wording. |

## Excluded claims

The narration cannot promise health benefit, diagnose a condition, describe a brain change as certain, state a treatment effect, make a claim about enlightenment, or rank scientific and Buddhist approaches. It cannot present a measured construct as the whole of a tradition.

[2]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5758421/ "Van Dam et al. (2018), Mind the Hype"
[3]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4471252/ "Amihai & Kozhevnikov (2015), Buddhist meditation traditions review"
