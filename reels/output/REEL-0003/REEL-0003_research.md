# Reel 0003 Research: रोज़मर्रा में ध्यान को कैसे पहचानें?

## Intended learning question

Reel 0003 addresses the deterministic registry question: **"हम इसे रोज़मर्रा में कैसे पहचानते हैं?"** Its narrow goal is to explain that concentrating on one task can make an unexpected detail easier to miss, while avoiding the inaccurate shortcut that people literally perceive nothing outside their focus.

## Verified source basis

| Source | Relevant finding | Allowed reel use |
|---|---|---|
| Kreitz, Furley, Memmert & Simons, *PLOS ONE* (2015), DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0134675 | People can fail to notice salient unexpected objects when attention is occupied; in the tested tasks, stable cognitive-ability measures were generally weak predictors of noticing. | Use an everyday example of missing an unexpected detail while focused; state that it can happen and is not a simple intelligence test. |
| van Boxtel, Tsuchiya & Koch, *Frontiers in Psychology* (2010), DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00217 | Review discusses attention and consciousness as dissociable in some paradigms and cautions that attention-related "blindness" does not usually mean total absence of perception; scene gist can remain available. | Explain that focus influences what people report noticing, but do not claim that the unattended world disappears or that one everyday example proves a theory of consciousness. |

## Evidence-safe claim rules for the script

1. Say **"कभी-कभी छूट सकता है"** rather than **"ज़रूर नहीं दिखेगा"**.
2. Present notification, conversation, or traffic examples as illustrations, not laboratory replications or safety advice.
3. Distinguish noticing a detail from an absolute statement about awareness or consciousness.
4. Do not infer personality, intelligence, ADHD, trauma, or diagnosis from an everyday attention lapse.
5. Close with a practical observation prompt, not medical, driving, or mental-health advice.

## Approved unique angle

**The “missed detail” is a cue to ask where focus went, not evidence that the rest of experience vanished.** This differs from Reel 0002, which focused on the conceptual distinction between attention and consciousness.
