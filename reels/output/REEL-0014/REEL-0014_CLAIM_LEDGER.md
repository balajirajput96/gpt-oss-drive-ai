# Reel 0014 Claim Ledger

| Time | Spoken claim | Evidence label | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–10s | A task score is not identical to “attention itself.” | Methodological finding | Tests can mix component processes; applies to measured task performance, not a diagnosis.[^1] |
| 10–23s | Similar task names can tap separable components. | Empirical result | Treviño et al. factor-analysed a particular battery and sample; not every task or population.[^1] |
| 23–36s | Attention has shared and task-specific components. | Empirical/model result | Yoo et al. modelled defined tasks and samples; it is not an individual clinical assessment.[^2] |
| 36–50s | Reports and performance each have different confounds for awareness research. | Review/methodological analysis | This does not prove a person is or is not conscious.[^3] |
| 50–60s | Use multiple measures and state what was actually measured. | Research-practice inference | General methodological recommendation, not medical advice. |

[^1]: Treviño, M. et al. (2021). *How do we measure attention?* https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-021-00313-1
[^2]: Yoo, K. et al. (2022). *A brain-based general measure of attention*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01301-1
[^3]: Jimenez, M. et al. (2024). *Consciousness Under the Spotlight*. https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1697
