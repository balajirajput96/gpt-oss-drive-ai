# Reel 0011 Claim Ledger

| Time | Spoken claim | Evidence label | Source and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–8s | “नाम सुनकर हमारा ध्यान कुछ features की ओर जा सकता है।” | Empirical result, task-specific | Barnhart et al. used an artificial-category eye-tracking paradigm; do not generalize to every real-world perception task.[^1] |
| 8–22s | “Shared label ने study में category-relevant features पर attention बढ़ाने से association दिखाया।” | Empirical result, task-specific | The study found label-associated attention optimization over learning; it does not show an immutable effect.[^1] |
| 22–37s | “तेज़ response का मतलब हमेशा raw seeing बदलना नहीं है।” | Empirical result plus alternative explanation | Klemfuss et al. found a linguistic visual-search advantage was removed when a cue reduced working-memory demand.[^2] |
| 37–49s | “एक framework language को flexible modulator मानता है।” | Theory/review | Label-feedback hypothesis is explanatory theory, not settled proof for all perception.[^3] |
| 49–59s | “पहले features देखो, फिर नाम सोचो; अपने focus को note करो।” | Reflection prompt | Not a diagnostic test, treatment, or personal inference. |

[^2]: Klemfuss, N., Prinzmetal, W., & Ivry, R. B. (2012). *How Does Language Change Perception: A Cautionary Note*. Frontiers in Psychology, 3, 78. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00078

[^3]: Lupyan, G. (2012). *Linguistically Modulated Perception and Cognition: The Label-Feedback Hypothesis*. Frontiers in Psychology, 3, 54. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00054
