# Reel 0005 Research: कौन-सा प्रमाण सबसे मजबूत है?

## Intended learning question

Reel 0005 answers the deterministic registry question **“कौन-सा प्रमाण सबसे मजबूत है?”** for inattentional blindness and selective attention. The answer is deliberately narrow: there is no single experiment that proves a universal everyday rule. The most useful support comes from **converging controlled experiments using clearly specified tasks**, interpreted alongside a systematic account of how different paradigms measure related but non-identical forms of the phenomenon.

## Evidence hierarchy for this reel

| Evidence source | What it can support | Evidence label | Critical limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simons & Chabris (1999), *Perception*, DOI 10.1068/p281059 | In one dynamic divided-attention task, noticing an unexpected object varied with its similarity to display objects and the difficulty of the primary monitoring task. | Primary controlled experiment | Supports the result in that task; it does not set a universal rate, diagnose anyone, or prove all everyday misses have the same cause. |
| Wood & Simons (2017), *Collabra: Psychology*, DOI 10.1525/collabra.90 | Three preregistered experiments used tracking displays to test how category- and feature-based attention sets related to noticing; in their task, filtering was category-based. | Primary experimental programme | Supports a specific account in the tested displays, not a final mechanism for every inattentional-blindness context. |
| Redlich, Memmert & Kreitz (2021), *Applied Cognitive Psychology*, DOI 10.1002/acp.3746 | A systematic methodological review covered 129 articles and 219 experiments, documenting varied paradigms and arguing that inattentional blindness likely includes subtypes with different mechanisms. | Systematic methodological synthesis | It maps research methods; it is not a pooled causal-effect estimate or proof that laboratory tasks directly transfer to every real-world setting. |
| Yale Perception & Cognition Lab reference guide | Terminology and literature discovery for published inattentional-blindness work. | Institutional research guide | It is not an experiment, review effect size, or a source for numerical claims. |

## Evidence-safe conclusion

> सबसे मजबूत बात किसी एक viral demonstration से नहीं निकलती। Controlled experiments हमें बताते हैं कि particular tasks में noticing task demand और attention set के साथ बदल सकती है; broad method reviews बताते हैं कि अलग paradigms अलग mechanisms और transfer limits ला सकते हैं. [1] [2] [3]

## Permitted claims

1. A controlled experiment gives the clearest answer about the conditions it actually tested.
2. Agreement or patterns across more than one well-specified task are more informative than one memorable demonstration.
3. A systematic review can show how broad and methodologically varied the evidence base is; it does not make all paradigms interchangeable.
4. Inattentional blindness research supports task- and stimulus-dependent conclusions, not intelligence, personality, diagnosis, or a statement that people literally do not see reality.

## Prohibited claims

- Do not call the gorilla experiment “final proof” or state a universal percentage of people who miss an unexpected object.
- Do not describe a systematic method review as a medical-style hierarchy that automatically proves causation.
- Do not generalize laboratory findings into driving, clinical, legal, or safety advice.
- Do not convert attention research into spiritual, philosophical, or diagnostic claims.
