# Reel 0015 Research — Alternative Explanations

## Deterministic scope

**Tuple:** `MND/L01/Q15`  
**Question:** कौन-सी वैकल्पिक व्याख्या संभव है?  
**Topic:** why an observed attention-related result may reflect more than the initially named process.

## Evidence boundaries

| Claim | Label | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Attention is not unitary and overlaps conceptually with working memory and cognitive control. | Review/theory | An observed task effect can have more than one cognitive interpretation; it does not settle which account is true.[^1] |
| Attention and decision-making can be experimentally confounded. | Empirical/methodological finding | Moerel et al. separated them using a specific two-stage design; this does not make every study invalid.[^2] |
| Causal conclusions require attention to manipulation and alternative causes. | Methodological analysis | Correlation and causal interpretation should not be treated as interchangeable.[^3] |

## Allowed script framing

The reel may say: “a result can fit more than one explanation,” then ask whether stimulus salience, memory demand, response preparation, decision requirement, task instruction, or sample/context could also contribute. It must not claim that any named alternative did cause a specific study outcome, diagnose an individual, or dismiss research wholesale.

## References

[^2]: Moerel, D., Rich, A. N., & Woolgar, A. (2024). [Selective Attention and Decision-Making Have Separable Neural Bases in Space and Time](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0224-24.2024).
[^3]: Dijkstra, N., & de Bruin, L. (2016). [Cognitive Neuroscience and Causal Inference](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2016.00129).
