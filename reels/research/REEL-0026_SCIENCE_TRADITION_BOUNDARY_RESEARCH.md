## Scope and answer boundary

The preceding Reel 0025 described selected Buddhist traditions as traditional or philosophical perspectives, not as one homogeneous system and not as laboratory proof. Reel 0026 answers the contrast carefully: **science converts a limited question into defined measures and studies those measures in a specified context**. It can investigate selected reports, tasks, behaviours, physiological signals, or brain measurements. It does not, by that method alone, settle a tradition’s ethical aims, liberation-oriented meaning, authority, or spiritual truth.

> “In scientific research, meaning is constructed based on two levels of definition: the conceptual and the operational.” — Phan-Le, Brennan & Parker, 2022 [1]

This is a methodological distinction, not a claim that scientific and traditional vocabularies are enemies, or that either one is superior.

## Directly supported source findings

| Source | What it supports for this reel | Limitation respected in narration |
|---|---|---|
| Phan-Le, Brennan & Parker (2022), PLOS ONE scoping review | Scientific work uses conceptual definitions and operational definitions: repeatable procedures for measuring a specified construct. The review finds no mindfulness operationalisation accepted across all research disciplines. [1] | Do not call one questionnaire, task, scan, or study the complete meaning of mindfulness or of a Buddhist practice. |
| Van Dam et al. (2018), *Perspectives on Psychological Science* | “Mindfulness” is not a unitary construct; studies and programs vary in training, participants, practice, and outcomes. The authors call for explicit, differentiated descriptions of the state, process, function, and practice studied. [2] | Do not turn a result from one intervention, population, or measure into a universal conclusion. Do not claim settled brain mechanisms or health outcomes. |
| Amihai & Kozhevnikov (2015), *Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine* review | Buddhist meditation traditions are described as diverse; the review distinguishes Theravada, Zen, and Vajrayana-related practices and their differing emphases. [3] | Do not compress Buddhist traditions into “attention training,” and do not represent a scholarly review of traditions as proof of doctrine. |

> “Mindfulness does not constitute a unitary construct.” — Van Dam et al., 2018 [2]

## Claims excluded from Reel 0026

The production must not say or imply that Buddhism is scientifically proven, disproven, reducible to brain activity, medically effective, universally calming, or superior/inferior to scientific inquiry. It must not cite effects, percentages, diagnostic advice, treatment outcomes, neural causation, enlightenment outcomes, or a universal definition of mindfulness. It must not say that science can decide spiritual truth, ethical worth, or a tradition’s final purpose.
