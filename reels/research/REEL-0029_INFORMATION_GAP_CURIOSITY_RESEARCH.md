# Reel 0029 — Information Gap और जिज्ञासा

**Registry identity:** `0029 / Batch_001 / MND / L01 / Q29`

**Required question:** “इससे जिज्ञासा कैसे बढ़ती है?”

## Scope

इस reel में “इससे” का अर्थ किसी विषय में महसूस होने वाला **information gap** है—यानी व्यक्ति को यह पता चलना कि वह क्या जानता है और क्या जानना चाहता है, उनके बीच अंतर है। इसका सीमित, evidence-bounded उत्तर है: कुछ controlled question-and-answer paradigms में ऐसा gap, खासकर जब उत्तर आंशिक रूप से जाना हुआ या महत्वपूर्ण लगे, अधिक curiosity ratings और answer देखने की इच्छा से जुड़ सकता है। यह हर व्यक्ति, हर gap, या हर learning setting के लिए guarantee नहीं है। [1] [2]

## Evidence record

| Source | Evidence type | What it supports | Boundary for this reel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kidd & Hayden (2015) | Peer-reviewed perspective/review | Curiosity has competing definitions; the information-gap account describes curiosity as arising from a perceived gap in knowledge. The review summarizes work where trivia-answer curiosity was highest at intermediate confidence. [1] | Review-level synthesis, not an individual prediction or a settled neural explanation. |
| Spitzer et al. (2023) | Two-experiment replication/extension | In the reported trivia paradigms, curiosity was highest at moderate confidence; perceived importance moderated the confidence–curiosity relation. [2] | A task-specific empirical pattern, not proof that a gap always motivates anyone. |
| Loewenstein (1994) | Theoretical review | The information-gap account frames curiosity as a felt discrepancy between knowledge and desired knowledge. [3] | A theory; use “may” and avoid treating the hunger metaphor as literal biology. |
| Dubey & Griffiths (2020) | Computational/theoretical model with experiments | A framework that relates curiosity to the value of information and future usefulness. [4] | Model-based account; do not claim any single variable determines curiosity. |

## Allowed claims

| Claim for narration | Evidence label | Wording constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Noticing a specific missing piece can sometimes make a question feel worth resolving. | Evidence-bounded empirical framing | Say “कभी-कभी” or “कुछ studies में”; never say “always.” [1] [2] |
| Intermediate confidence can be associated with higher curiosity ratings in trivia tasks. | Empirical finding from cited task paradigms | Do not assign a fixed confidence percentage or prescribe a personal threshold. [1] [2] |
| Perceived importance can change the relationship between confidence and curiosity. | Empirical finding | Do not claim importance guarantees engagement or learning. [2] |
| Information-gap theory is one account of curiosity. | Theory | Explicitly call it a “model/account,” not a universal fact. [3] [4] |
| Curiosity and information seeking are broader, overlapping concepts. | Review framing | Do not imply that every click, exploration, or question proves intrinsic curiosity. [1] |

## Prohibited overclaims

The reel must not claim that a curiosity gap manipulates people, guarantees study motivation, improves memory for every learner, creates dopamine on demand, diagnoses attention problems, gives a learning prescription, or proves a neural or spiritual explanation of curiosity. It must not use engagement statistics, a “perfect gap” formula, or a numerical confidence cutoff. Curiosity is not treated as a universal positive state; context, perceived importance, novelty, incentives, and individual differences can all matter. [1] [2] [4]

## Planned narrative boundary

The working Hindi framing is: “जब किसी सवाल में आपको थोड़ा संदर्भ मिलता है, पर उत्तर पूरा नहीं, तो एक missing piece महसूस हो सकती है। कुछ trivia studies में ऐसी partial knowing के साथ curiosity ratings अधिक दिखीं। पर बहुत कम जानकारी, बहुत अधिक certainty, या महत्व न लगने पर pattern बदल सकता है।” This is a scientific-summary framing, not an instruction to engineer attention.
