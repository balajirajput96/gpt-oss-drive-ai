# Reel 0028 — कौन-सा डेटा अभी गायब है?

**Registry identity:** `0028 / Batch_001 / MND / L01 / Q28`

**Research status:** Evidence-bounded measurement framing. This is not a claim that consciousness is unscientific, a diagnosis, or an assessment of any individual.

## Scope and bounded answer

The important missing data is a **direct, independently checkable ground-truth readout of another person’s subjective experience**. Researchers can collect first-person reports, observable behaviour, and brain signals; each can provide useful evidence, but none by itself is a complete, theory-free “experience meter.” This is a measurement boundary, not evidence that subjective experience does not exist or that brain research is futile. [1] [2]

> Current consciousness research connects first-person reports with third-person observations; it does not yet possess an instrument that converts a person’s lived experience directly into a universally validated external measurement. [1]

## Directly supported source findings

| Source | What it supports for this reel | Limitation retained |
|---|---|---|
| Jimenez et al. (2024), *WIREs Cognitive Science* | Review of subjective/report-based and objective/performance-based awareness measures. It explains that reports can be affected by response criteria and that task performance is an indirect measure of awareness. [1] | A review of measurement challenges; it does not demonstrate that any single neural or behavioural measure is useless. |
| Gamez (2014), *Frontiers in Psychology* | A scientific framework discussion: consciousness measurements rely on first-person reports and explicit working assumptions when relating experience to physical data. [2] | This is a theoretical framework paper, not a clinical rule or proof for any metaphysical view. |
| Duman et al. (2022), *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* | Report tasks may contain attention, decision, and metacognitive processes; no-report designs also need validation and have alternative interpretations. [3] | The article critiques methodology; it does not establish that all report or no-report studies fail. |
| Walter (2021), *Neuroscience of Consciousness* | In some disorders-of-consciousness contexts, behavioural and neural measures face a lack of direct ground truth and may be sensitive to different dimensions of experience. [4] | This clinical discussion must not be used to diagnose, prognose, or advise about patients. |
| Pitts, Lutsyshyna & Hillyard (2018), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* | The relationship between attention and consciousness is contested and depends on operational definitions and study design. [5] | Do not say attention and consciousness are identical, wholly separate, or resolved by one experiment. |

## Excluded claims

The reel must not claim that scanners can read every thought, that EEG/fMRI proves or disproves consciousness in an individual, that no scientific method can study experience, that any theory has solved consciousness, or that attention equals consciousness. It must not prescribe, diagnose, prognose, or discuss treatment for disorders of consciousness. It must not use a numerical accuracy claim, a sensational “mind-reading” claim, or a spiritual/metaphysical conclusion as if it were empirical evidence.

## Script-production requirements

The script should begin with the bounded hook: **“अगर brain scan, behaviour और report तीनों हैं, तो क्या missing है?”** It must answer: **“किसी दूसरे व्यक्ति के अंदर के अनुभव का direct ground truth.”** It should label reports, behaviour, and brain signals as complementary clues; explain in ordinary language that each involves interpretation; and close with the distinction between evidence and certainty. Use only abstract, text-free original visuals; captions are composited separately. The reel must not show a medical patient, clinical monitor, real person, or diagnostic claim.
