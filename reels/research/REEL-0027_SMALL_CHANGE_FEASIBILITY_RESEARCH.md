## Scope and bounded answer

A small change can be a **workable starting design**, but it is not automatically feasible for every person, behaviour, or situation. The evidence reviewed here supports a modest framing: define one simple action, attach it to a clear recurring context, and observe what happens. It does not establish a universal timeline, guarantee motivation, diagnose a barrier, or prescribe an intervention.

> “Mere repetition of a simple action in a consistent context” can, through associative learning, make later action more likely to be cued by that context. — Gardner, Lally & Wardle, 2012 [1]

The same review notes that automaticity developed more quickly for simple actions than for more elaborate routines in the cited real-world study. This supports **task-specific plausibility**, not an assurance that “small habits always work.” [1]

> An implementation intention can be stated as: “If I encounter situation Y, then I will initiate action Z.” — Wieber, Thürmer & Gollwitzer, 2015 [2]

## Excluded claims

The reel must not promise habit formation, motivation, productivity, weight or health effects; give a fixed number of days; say missed repetition ruins or never affects progress; treat any barrier as lack of willpower; diagnose mental-health concerns; or imply that an if–then plan works without adaptation. It must not make causal brain claims.
