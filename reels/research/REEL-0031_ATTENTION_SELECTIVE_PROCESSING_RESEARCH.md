## Scope

The everyday phrase “ध्यान लगाना” can sound like one inner switch. The evidence record therefore uses a narrower working description: **in cognitive neuroscience, attention refers to a family of processes that can select, modulate, and sustain priority for information that is relevant to a task or goal.** Reviews distinguish externally supplied sensory information from internally generated information such as task rules or memory, and distinguish alerting, orienting, and executive-control functions. [1] [2]

This is a useful evidence-based simplification, not a claim that researchers have established one single attention system or one exhaustive definition. A published conceptual critique argues that the term is used for too many phenomena to safely treat it as a unitary construct. [3]

## Evidence record

| Chun, Golomb, and Turk-Browne taxonomy | Peer-reviewed conceptual review | Attention can select, modulate, and maintain both external sensory information and internal information such as task rules, responses, long-term memory, and working memory. [1] | Do not claim a person can always choose attention at will, or that every type of attention is the same process. |
| Petersen and Posner review | Peer-reviewed neuroscience review | The review organizes findings into alerting, orienting, and executive networks; orienting prioritizes sensory input by location or modality. [2] | Present the network labels as one influential framework, not as a complete map of every mental function or proof about an individual brain. |
| Yantis review | Peer-reviewed neuroscience review | In visual research, selection can be shaped both by stimulus properties and by behavioral goals; selection can concern locations, features, or objects. [4] | Do not say distraction is a moral failure, that attention blocks all irrelevant input, or that brain regions act in isolation. |
| Koch and Tsuchiya article | Peer-reviewed theoretical review of psychophysical evidence | The authors argue that top-down attention and consciousness can be experimentally dissociated and need not be identical. [5] | State the distinction cautiously: attention and conscious awareness are related but not interchangeable in this literature; do not claim the debate is settled across all meanings of consciousness. |
| Hommel and colleagues critique | Scholarly conceptual argument | “Attention” has many meanings and should not automatically be treated as a single coherent mechanism. [3] | Attribute this as a critique/proposal, not as a universal consensus. |

## Allowed claims

The narration may say that attention is commonly studied as a way of giving some information more priority than competing information. It may use a practical example: in a crowded room, a person may prioritize one voice, a visual location, or a task rule—without claiming that all other sounds or thoughts vanish. It may explain that something bright or sudden can pull priority, while goals can also guide selection. It may say that researchers separate related functions such as alertness, orienting, and control. [1] [2] [4]

The narration may correct the misconception that “attention” simply means awareness or deep concentration. It may say that scientific discussions distinguish these terms and that one review argues attention and consciousness can be experimentally dissociated. That statement must retain the qualifier **“some research frameworks/reviews”** or equivalent. [5]

## Prohibited overclaims

The narration must not equate attention with intelligence, willpower, meditation skill, moral worth, or “brain power.” It must not diagnose or screen for ADHD, anxiety, depression, dementia, or any other condition. It must not promise productivity, learning gains, exam performance, or an ability to eliminate distractions. It must not use the historical Stroop or attentional-blink packages as evidence or media. It must not claim that one brain region “is attention,” that attention has one settled definition, or that consciousness is fully explained or unrelated to attention. No numerical performance claim or neuroscience statistic may be introduced.

## Planned narrative boundary

The Hindi reel will open with the misconception **“क्या ध्यान का मतलब बस बहुत देर तक concentrate रहना है?”** It will then answer the immutable question in plain language: **ध्यान को एक चयन-प्रक्रिया की तरह समझिए—दिमाग कुछ जानकारी को उस समय ज्यादा प्राथमिकता देता है क्योंकि वह लक्ष्य, जगह, चीज़ या संकेत से जुड़ी है।** It will clarify that alertness, selecting a location, and controlling a response are related but not identical research functions. The final takeaway will be: **ध्यान कोई अकेला on/off switch नहीं; यह सीमित जानकारी में प्राथमिकता बदलने वाली कई प्रक्रियाओं का नाम है।** This is editorial synthesis from the cited reviews, not a clinical claim or a complete theory of consciousness.
