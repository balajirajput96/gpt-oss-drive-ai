# Reel 0014 Research — Measurement Limits

## Deterministic scope

**Registry tuple:** `MND/L01/Q14`  
**Question:** नापने में कठिनाई क्या है?  
**Working topic:** attention and subjective experience measurement: what a task score or brain signal can—and cannot—support.

## Evidence-bounded claims

| Claim | Evidence label | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| An “attention test” can combine more than one cognitive process. | Empirical/methodological finding | Treviño et al. report that common tests can load on separable components and that some nominal attention tests should not be classed as attention tests.[^1] |
| Attention is multifaceted, so a single task does not automatically measure overall attention. | Empirical interpretation | Yoo et al. state there is no singular neural measure of overall attention across tasks; their work models shared and task-specific components in defined samples.[^2] |
| Subjective reports and performance measures offer different, imperfect windows onto awareness. | Review/methodological analysis | Jimenez et al. describe report and performance approaches with distinct confounds; neither is a direct “consciousness meter.”[^3] |
| A score, reaction time, or scan is not a diagnosis or direct readout of a person’s mind. | Safety boundary | This reel makes no individual inference, clinical assessment, neural decoding, or universal claim. |

## Planned distinction for script

The reel will describe **what a measure records** (for example, task accuracy, reaction time, report, or a modeled brain pattern), then name **what else can influence it** (motor demands, memory, task design, response criterion, population and context). It will frame triangulating multiple measures as a research practice, not a method for diagnosing an individual.
