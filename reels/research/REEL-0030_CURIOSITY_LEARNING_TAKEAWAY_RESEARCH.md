## Scope

The allowed takeaway is deliberately narrow: **in controlled research, state curiosity has been associated with stronger memory for some material, but it is not a universal learning switch or a guaranteed instructional method.** The record treats the PACE account as a proposed framework, not a settled neural explanation. It also distinguishes self-reported curiosity, information-seeking behavior, and learning outcomes.

## Evidence record

| Evidence | Label | What it supports | Boundary for the reel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trivia-paradigm studies reviewed in PACE | Empirical pattern summarized in a review | Higher curiosity for particular trivia answers has been associated with better immediate and delayed memory in those paradigms. [1] | Do not turn this into a guarantee for every subject, learner, platform, or classroom. |
| Incidental-face findings reviewed in PACE | Empirical pattern summarized in a review | Some studies found better recognition for faces encountered while participants anticipated answers to high-curiosity trivia questions. [1] | Do not claim that curiosity automatically improves all surrounding information. |
| PACE | Proposed explanatory framework | PACE offers testable predictions connecting prediction, appraisal, curiosity, exploration, and memory. [1] | “May involve” and “framework proposes” are required; no direct dopamine or brain-region claim as fact for an individual. |
| Curiosity field overview | Review boundary | Curiosity lacks a single agreed definition, and laboratory tasks/manipulations are not standardized. [2] | A click, search, rating, or gaze measure alone must not be treated as conclusive proof of curiosity. |
| Two-experiment confidence/importance study | Empirical finding | The reported confidence–curiosity pattern replicated in its task materials; perceived importance moderated the relation. [3] | Do not give a fixed ideal confidence level or reduce curiosity to a single information-gap rule. |

## Allowed claims

The narration may say that research **suggests** curiosity can support attention and memory in particular experimental tasks. It may say that the strongest practical takeaway is to distinguish an intriguing question from a proven learning intervention. It may say that value, prior knowledge, confidence, task design, and context can matter. It may identify PACE as a framework that generates testable predictions.

## Prohibited overclaims

The narration must not promise improved exam scores, long-term memory, motivation, productivity, or mental health. It must not prescribe a “curiosity hack,” assert that a gap triggers dopamine on demand, identify a universal optimal uncertainty level, infer curiosity from clicking, or diagnose ADHD, anxiety, or any other condition. No numerical effect size or population statistic may be added unless directly supported and contextualized.

## Planned narrative boundary

The Hindi reel will begin with the apparent contradiction—“क्या जिज्ञासा पढ़ाई को अपने-आप आसान बना देती है?”—then correct it: lab results can be useful clues, not a guarantee. It will name a bounded main learning: **अच्छा सवाल ध्यान को खोल सकता है; सीखने का परिणाम फिर भी व्यक्ति, सामग्री और अभ्यास पर निर्भर रहता है।** This is an editorial synthesis of the reviewed evidence, not an experimental result or medical advice.
