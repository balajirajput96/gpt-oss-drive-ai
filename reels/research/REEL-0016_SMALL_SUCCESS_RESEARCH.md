# Reel 0016 Research — Why Small Success Can Matter

## Deterministic scope

**Tuple:** `MND/L01/Q16`  
**Question:** छोटी सफलता क्यों मायने रखती है?  
**Topic:** task-specific mastery feedback and self-efficacy, not a universal “small wins” mechanism.

## Evidence boundaries

| Claim | Label | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Self-efficacy is a task- and situation-specific belief about capability, not identical to actual skill. | Theory/review | A small success in one setting does not prove competence or transfer everywhere.[^1] |
| Enactive mastery experiences are described as influential information for self-efficacy beliefs. | Theory/review | This does not guarantee sustained motivation, learning, or behavior change for every person.[^1] |
| Feedback and goal progress can help learners evaluate performance. | Educational implication | Feedback should be honest and tied to task performance, not empty praise.[^1] |
| Intervention effects can vary substantially across settings and studies. | Systematic-review limitation | A health-education meta-analysis showed high heterogeneity; it is not evidence about every learning context.[^2] |

## Allowed script framing

The reel may say that a small, real, task-specific success can provide information about what worked and support a next attempt. It must say that context, feedback, skill, and goal difficulty matter. It must not claim a dopamine release, guaranteed habit formation, treatment effect, intelligence increase, or that “any small win” transforms motivation.

## References

[^1]: Artino, A. R. (2012). [Academic self-efficacy: from educational theory to instructional practice](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-012-0012-5).
[^2]: Busabong, W. et al. (2025). [Impact of self-efficacy-based health education programs on behavior modification](https://doi.org/10.3347/PHD.24074).
