# Reel 0023 Research Record — Identity, Role, and Attention

**Registry identity:** `0023 / Batch_001 / MND / L01 / Q23`
**Question:** यह पहचान और भूमिका से कैसे जुड़ता है?

## Evidence-grounded angle

Attention is not a fixed statement of who a person is. In controlled social-psychology studies, however, a currently salient group identity or an assigned role can shape what participants attend to and remember in a task. Van Bavel and Cunningham reported that social identity shaped attention and memory across three experiments, and that assigning participants a role requiring attention to out-group members changed the observed memory pattern. [1]

Berkman, Livingston, and Kahn describe the **identity-value model** as a theoretical account that connects core values, group affiliations, self-relevance, and dynamic value integration in goal-directed behaviour. The article explicitly presents hypotheses and boundary conditions rather than a test that can read an individual’s identity or predict a particular decision. [2]

Montalan and colleagues found in two intergroup evaluative-task experiments that identity-based motivational context could influence an attention-related ERP measure. This is laboratory evidence about a restricted task and sample; it is not a basis for diagnosing bias, inferring motives, or claiming that any role inevitably controls attention. [3]

> The 2015 article *Looking for my self: Identity-driven attention allocation* is marked **retracted** by the publisher. It is excluded from this reel’s evidence base and must not be cited, paraphrased as support, or used in the script. [4]

## Permitted production statements

| Statement class | Permitted scope | Evidence label |
|---|---|---|
| Identity can be contextually salient | Different aspects of self and group membership may become relevant in a task or situation. | Theory / contextual framing |
| Roles can guide task-relevant attention | An assigned role changed attention/memory patterns in an experimental social task. | Experimental finding |
| Identity relevance can be part of goal valuation | The identity-value model proposes identity as one input among several to subjective value and self-regulation. | Theoretical model |
| Viewer reflection | “किस भूमिका में मैं क्या देख रहा/रही हूँ?” can be posed as a reflective question. | Practical reflection, not a test |

## Prohibited overclaims

| Do not claim | Why it is outside evidence |
|---|---|
| “Your role decides what you notice.” | The evidence is contextual and task-specific, not deterministic. |
| “Attention reveals your true identity.” | Attention is not a diagnostic identity test. |
| “Group identity always causes prejudice or bias.” | The cited studies do not support universal causal claims about individuals. |
| “Change your identity to cure distraction.” | No clinical treatment or guaranteed intervention claim is supported. |
| “Brain signals prove a person’s motives.” | The ERP study does not provide individual motive-reading. |
