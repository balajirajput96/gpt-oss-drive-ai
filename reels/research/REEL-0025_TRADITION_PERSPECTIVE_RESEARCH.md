# Reel 0025 Research Record — A Buddhist Contemplative Perspective on Attention

**Registry identity:** `0025 / Batch_001 / MND / L01 / Q25`
**Question:** इस परंपरा का दृष्टिकोण क्या है?
**Selected scope:** An **Indo-Tibetan Buddhist and related Buddhist contemplative perspective** on attention/mindfulness. This is a bounded tradition-specific interpretation, not a claim that every Buddhist school or practitioner holds the same view.

## Scholarly sources and findings

Amihai and Kozhevnikov’s 2015 review describes differences across Buddhist meditation traditions rather than one unified account. Its discussion characterizes Theravada practices such as shamatha, vipassana, and mindfulness as approaches that emphasize internally steadying the mind and cultivating quiescence/tranquility; it contrasts these descriptions with other Buddhist traditions, including Mahayana and Vajrayana. [1]

> “While meditative techniques of all Buddhist teachings stress liberation from all conceptual delusions, the means of achieving it are quite different.” — Amihai & Kozhevnikov, 2015 [1]

Thupten’s 2019 **opinion paper** adds the Indo-Tibetan Buddhist perspective to debate about contemporary mindfulness. It places `sati` and `satipaṭṭhāna` in a larger context of morality, concentration, and wisdom, and argues that secular mindfulness can be separated from that historical ethical and soteriological context. This is a scholarly interpretation and argument, not empirical evidence that a particular practice produces a specific outcome. [2]

## Prohibited claims

The reel must not state that Buddhism proves a scientific model, that meditation cures or prevents a condition, that all Buddhist schools teach the same method, that a viewer should adopt a religion, or that attention training guarantees calm, awakening, compassion, morality, or any clinical outcome. It must not mix up historical religious aims with experimental results.
