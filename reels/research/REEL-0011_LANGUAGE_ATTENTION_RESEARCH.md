## Evidence-backed core

> **Working answer:** किसी task में label या नाम relevant features पर attention को guide कर सकता है और categorization को आसान बना सकता है। लेकिन इससे यह निष्कर्ष नहीं निकलता कि भाषा हर स्थिति में raw sensory experience को सीधे बदल देती है। Working memory, task demands, response selection और learned categories जैसे alternative mechanisms भी explanation का हिस्सा हो सकते हैं.

Barnhart, Rivera, and Robinson used eye tracking with eight-year-olds and adults learning artificial sparse categories. In their task, a shared label was associated with increased attention to category-relevant features over time, whereas unique labels produced a different attentional pattern. This supports a **task-specific, label-guided attention** claim, not a universal claim about perception.[^barnhart]

Klemfuss, Prinzmetal, and Ivry tested a linguistic advantage in visual search and found that the advantage disappeared when a visible cue reduced working-memory demands. Their experiments therefore demonstrate that a language-related performance difference can plausibly arise after early sensing, for example through memory or response-selection processes. The authors explicitly caution against treating visual-search performance alone as proof that perception itself changed.[^klemfuss]

Lupyan’s label-feedback paper is a **theoretical review/hypothesis** proposing that labels may flexibly modulate ongoing cognitive and perceptual processing. It is not single-study proof that language deterministically controls perception. Its most useful role here is to distinguish an explanatory framework from the direct experimental findings.[^lupyan]

## Claim ledger

| Proposed reel claim | Evidence class | Safe wording | Do not say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labels can help guide attention to diagnostic features during a learning task. | Direct empirical result in a specific paradigm | “कुछ tasks में नाम हमारा ध्यान relevant clues की ओर खींच सकते हैं।” | “शब्द हमेशा वही दिखाते हैं जो वे कहते हैं।” |
| A faster language-cued visual-search response need not prove altered sensation. | Direct empirical result plus alternative-process interpretation | “तेज़ response memory या decision strategy से भी आ सकता है।” | “भाषा ने आँखों की raw seeing बदल दी।” |
| Language may flexibly modulate task processing. | Theory/review | “एक proposed framework यह संभावना रखता है…” | “Neuroscience ने साबित कर दिया कि language perception को नियंत्रित करती है।” |
| Daily self-check | Educational reflection, not evidence test | “पहले object के features बताइए, फिर उसका नाम सोचिए—क्या आपका focus बदला?” | “इससे अपना brain type diagnose करें।” |

## Planned Hindi script boundary

1. **Hook:** “क्या किसी चीज़ का नाम सुनते ही आपका ध्यान बदल सकता है?”
2. Explain that labels can work like an attention cue in a particular learning task.
3. Give a text-free, ordinary-object visual comparison without claiming the label changes the object.
4. State the important limit: a faster response can reflect memory/decision demands, not necessarily raw perception.
5. Close with a low-risk observation prompt and source note.
